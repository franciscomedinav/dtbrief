DoorDash Is Buying Instacart for $17 Billion. Here’s What Changes for Your Deliveries.

The restaurant-delivery giant is paying $65 a share for the grocery-delivery leader — a deal that would put dinner and your weekly shop under one roof, if regulators let it through.

1 What happened

DoorDash has agreed to buy Instacart for $17 billion, paying $65 per share — 26 percent more than Instacart’s share price on October 22. The deal unites two companies that built their empires in different aisles of the same business: DoorDash in restaurant delivery, Instacart in groceries.

The companies say combining their technology and infrastructure will mean faster deliveries and more affordable prices. But nothing changes for customers right away. The deal is not expected to close until late next year or in 2028, which gives regulators a long runway to examine it — and gives rivals time to respond.

2 Why it matters

First, delivery is consolidating. A combined company would rival Amazon, Walmart, and Uber Eats. Scale matters in delivery because it spreads the cost of drivers, warehouses, and software across more orders — and the biggest player can often afford to charge less, or deliver faster, than everyone else.

Dinner and the weekly shop, from the same app, by the same courier network. That is the $17 billion bet.

Second, the deal tests how regulators view a market that feels competitive to shoppers but is concentrated behind the scenes. There are many apps on your phone, but only a handful of companies with the logistics to fulfil millions of orders a day. Expect the review to focus on prices, courier pay, and whether restaurants and grocers lose bargaining power when two big buyers become one.

Third, for couriers and small businesses, the fine print matters more than the headline. A combined company could mean more work in one app — or tougher terms, as a single platform gains leverage over the restaurants and stores that depend on it. The promises of lower prices and faster delivery will be judged against what actually happens to fees and pay.

3 What to watch next

  • Regulators Watch for antitrust review in the US and beyond. A long close timeline means the deal could be reshaped, delayed, or blocked before it ever reaches customers.
  • Rivals respond Amazon, Walmart, and Uber Eats will not stand still. Expect grocery and restaurant partnerships, price moves, and membership perks aimed at holding on to shoppers.
  • Fees and pay The real test of the “more affordable” promise: delivery fees, service charges, and courier pay in the months after any close, not the press release on day one.
  • The combined app Watch whether DoorDash merges the two services into one app, keeps Instacart as a separate brand, or lands somewhere in between — and what happens to Instacart’s retail partners.
