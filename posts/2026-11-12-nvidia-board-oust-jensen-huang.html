Nvidia’s Board Moves to Oust Jensen Huang.

The board met Wednesday night to decide the fate of the co-founder who built the world’s most valuable chipmaker — after two affairs, one with a suspected Chinese spy, and a stock slide that has erased about $600 billion in value.

1 What happened

Nvidia’s board is preparing to oust chief executive Jensen Huang after two extramarital affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy, the Wall Street Journal reported Wednesday evening. The board met late Wednesday to decide his future, a source told Reuters, and shares fell 8.6 percent after hours to $158.76, after closing up 2.9 percent at $173.73. As of Thursday morning, no decision had been announced.

On Sunday, Huang told staff in a letter that he had been unfaithful to his wife, apologizing for violating his family’s and his employees’ trust. The woman at the center of the espionage allegations is Alice Huang, a top executive at Singapore-based data-center company Megaspeed International, which is under scrutiny over allegations it moved restricted Nvidia chips to China. The Justice Department is now leading multiple investigations involving her, and China has denied the spy allegations. On November 8, the board opened an independent investigation, led by outside counsel Wachtell Lipton, into whether Huang had been targeted by foreign intelligence.

Then on Wednesday, the New York Times reported a second affair, this one inside the company: Huang had a relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s vice president of health care, who reports directly to him, citing five people who worked at the company. Two of them said the pair were seen being affectionate at a San Jose hotel during Nvidia’s GTC conference in March; the Times said it has not seen the video, and neither Huang nor Powell has confirmed the relationship. Nvidia’s code of conduct warns against personal relationships between a manager and a subordinate.

Washington is now involved on both sides of the aisle. Senators Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons asked the Trump administration to preserve all documents tied to the chip exports and the affair. Three Republican House committee chairmen want Huang to brief them on November 18, and say they may subpoena him. Under Nvidia’s bylaws, a majority of its 11 directors can remove Huang with or without cause.

2 Why it matters

First, this is a leadership crisis at the company the entire AI boom runs on. Nvidia dominates AI processors, and Huang has been its face and strategist for more than three decades. The board’s own proxy statement warns the company is highly dependent on its longstanding executive team, and Huang has said he does not believe in succession planning. There is no obvious successor, and the market is pricing that in: if the drop holds, Nvidia will be down 23.2 percent from its November 3 close, with about $600 billion gone since the probe began.

Second, the scandal is now two stories at once: governance and national security. The Powell affair is a governance problem — a chief executive involved with a direct report, against the company’s own policy. The Alice Huang affair is a national-security problem: a CEO who lobbied the president to let Nvidia ship chips to China while involved with a business partner US officials believe is a Chinese intelligence asset. The investigation asks whether secrets were compromised; the ouster question is whether the man at the top can still be trusted.

One affair is a governance problem. The other is a national-security problem. The board has to decide whether either one — or both together — means the co-founder has to go.

Third, the fallout reaches beyond Nvidia. Nvidia reports results on November 17, and investors will watch chip demand while the leadership question hangs open. It is also a stress test for the whole AI trade: a Federal Reserve governor warned this week that disappointing AI returns could trigger a large correction, and the boom’s central chipmaker losing its founder-CEO in a scandal is exactly that kind of shock.

The rest of today’s briefing

A Federal Reserve governor warns that disappointing AI returns could trigger a large stock-market correction — Governor Michelle Bowman told the Council on Foreign Relations that stock prices could face a large correction if returns on AI investments fall short of expectations. AI-related debt is on track to exceed $800 billion this year, with off-balance-sheet obligations carrying potential payments of more than three times that amount, and AI revenues still do not cover the cost of maintaining models and building data centers. She also said inflation has disappointed, with twelve-month core inflation likely rising to 3.3 percent in October. — Bloomberg Law

OpenAI’s quarterly revenue crossed $10 billion for the first time, with enterprise sales doubling — OpenAI’s quarterly revenue hit $10 billion in the third quarter, up almost 50 percent from $6.7 billion in the second, reaching a $50 billion annual run rate. For the first time, revenue is split almost evenly between enterprise and consumer customers, with enterprise doubling quarter over quarter. But the adjusted operating margin is still minus 109 percent, and the operating loss widened to $10.9 billion. The figures have not been confirmed by OpenAI. — TechCrunch

Google DeepMind unveils AlphaGenome 2, an AI model that reads DNA variant effects cell by cell — DeepMind announced AlphaGenome 2, a DNA sequence model that predicts how genetic variants affect gene activity in individual human cell types, rather than tissue averages. Trained on more than 107 million cell profiles across 68 tissues, it identifies nearly twice as many gene-expression-changing variants as its predecessor — 30 percent of causal variants on a 28-immune-cell-type test, versus 16 percent. It is available now for non-commercial research, and for research only, not clinical use. — Google DeepMind

IBM unveils its Nighthawk r3 quantum processor and a plan to link two chips into a 1,040-qubit system in 2027 — IBM announced Nighthawk r3, designed to execute 10,000 quantum operations across 520 programmable qubits, and plans to connect two of them into a single 1,040-qubit system in 2027. It is a roadmap, not a working machine: no pricing, no availability date, and no claim of fault-tolerant computing. The current r2 chip, introduced in September, runs more than 7,500 operations on 120 qubits, and IBM is targeting its fault-tolerant Starling system for 2029. — Interesting Engineering

United Airlines becomes the first major US carrier to ban AI agents from booking its flights — United rewrote its Contract of Carriage to bar personal AI agents from making, modifying, or servicing tickets, and lets it cancel reservations made by AI agents without notice. The ban covers its website, app, travel agencies, and booking platforms. AI tools can still search flights and show fares, but you must complete the purchase yourself, and bookings made before November 11 will not be cancelled. CEO Scott Kirby said he does not want to create another middleman. — USA Today

Runway open-sources Praxis-1, a 27-billion-parameter robot model that learned to act by watching video — Runway released Praxis-1 with open weights under the Apache 2.0 license, which allows commercial use. The world action model predicts a short video clip of what should happen next and derives the robot’s actions from that imagined future. On Nvidia’s RoboLab-120 benchmark it completed 555 of 1,200 episodes, a 46.2 percent success rate, ahead of rival models — though the numbers are Runway’s own, and 46 percent still means failing more than half the time. — Runway

CoreWeave turns to customer prepayments and shorter contracts to fund its AI buildout — CoreWeave is asking customers to pay up front, borrowing against specific contracts, and chasing short-term deals at higher prices. It expects about $9 billion in prepayments and deposits over the next three months, and will not use the unsecured debt market at current yields, its CEO said. Revenue hit $3.66 billion, up 168 percent, but the company posted a $709 million net loss and $846 million in quarterly interest expense. — TechCrunch

Google launches the Gemini Decisions API, a model that answers up to 1,000 questions in a single pass without writing text — Google launched the Gemini Decisions API, powered by Gemini Decision 1, which scores candidate options in one forward pass and returns probabilities in 150 to 250 milliseconds. One request can carry up to 1,000 questions against a single multimodal input of up to 256,000 tokens. It targets tool selection, policy checks, and model routing inside AI agents, at 10 cents per million input tokens with zero output tokens billed. — VentureBeat

Apple’s Claris is testing Harbor, an app that turns Macs into a private local AI server — Apple’s Claris subsidiary is testing Harbor, an app that runs open-weight language models locally on Apple silicon so data never leaves your hardware. It can link two or more Thunderbolt 5 Macs into a cluster that pools unified memory to run larger models. Harbor is in private preview, an App Store listing briefly appeared on November 10 before Apple removed it, and there is no price or release date. — MacRumors
