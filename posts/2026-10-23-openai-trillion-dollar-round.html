OpenAI Is About to Be Worth $1.25 Trillion. Here’s What That Actually Means.

A funding round of nearly $30 billion, led by investors from the United Arab Emirates, would put the maker of ChatGPT in the same league as Apple and Microsoft — before it has ever turned a profit.

1 What happened

CNBC reported on Thursday that OpenAI is closing in on a new funding round of nearly $30 billion, and that the deal would value the company at roughly $1.25 trillion. Investors from the United Arab Emirates are leading the round.

In plain English: a group of very wealthy investors is about to hand OpenAI an enormous pile of cash, and in exchange they get a slice of the company. The $1.25 trillion figure is the price tag the deal puts on the whole business — if OpenAI were sold outright tomorrow at that price, that’s what it would fetch.

To feel how big that is: only a handful of companies on Earth are worth more than a trillion dollars, and all of them — Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia — sell their shares on the public stock market. OpenAI would join that club while still being a private company most people know simply as “the ChatGPT company.”

2 Why it matters

First, AI runs on money before it runs on anything else. Training and running advanced models takes warehouses full of specialized chips, and those data centers cost staggering amounts to build and power. A $30 billion round is, more than anything, fuel for that construction project.

Second, look at who’s writing the checks. Gulf states have spent years converting oil wealth into technology stakes, and leading a round this size buys more than a financial return — it buys a seat at the table in the AI era, and a say in where the next generation of data centers gets built.

A private company that has never turned a profit is now priced like Apple. That tells you less about OpenAI’s present than about how completely investors believe AI is the future.

Third, the number resets the bar for everyone else. OpenAI’s rivals now have to compete against a company holding a war chest of this size — in the same week, China’s DeepSeek raised roughly $15 billion of its own. For the rest of us, the practical effect is simpler: flush with cash, these companies keep racing to ship better models and features, and keep prices low while the land grab lasts.

3 What to watch next

  • The close “Closing in on” is not “closed.” Watch whether the round actually lands at the full reported size and valuation — deals this big can shrink or slip.
  • The build-out Expect announcements about where the money goes: new data centers and chip deals, quite possibly including major projects in the Gulf itself.
  • Washington Sovereign wealth from abroad flowing into America’s most strategically important AI company, at this scale, is exactly the kind of deal that draws government attention.
  • The IPO question A private valuation this large can’t grow forever in private markets. Pressure will build for OpenAI to sell shares to the public — and to prove its revenue can eventually justify the price tag.
