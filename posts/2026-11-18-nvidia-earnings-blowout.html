Nvidia Crushes Earnings Without Huang.

Revenue jumped 103% to $115.8 billion in Nvidia’s first report since Jensen Huang resigned — and the board says it has no plans to bring him back.

1 What happened

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter results after the market closed on Tuesday, November 17, and the numbers were extraordinary by any measure. Revenue hit $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and about $7 billion above what Wall Street expected, while adjusted earnings came in at $2.68 a share against a consensus of about $2.50. Net income more than doubled to $65.3 billion, and gross margin held at 74.9% on an adjusted basis. The data center business, the engine of the AI boom, surged 110% to $107.6 billion — nearly 93% of total sales — and the company forecast about $130 billion in revenue for the current quarter, above the roughly $125 billion analysts expected.

The report also showed the company’s newest hardware is already in customers’ hands: shipments of the Vera Rubin NVL72 system, Nvidia’s latest AI computing platform, are running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle, and Nebius. Nvidia returned $28.1 billion to shareholders through buybacks and dividends during the quarter. But the outlook carried a warning, too. The fourth-quarter forecast assumes no data center revenue from China at all, and interim chief executive Colette Kress confirmed that licensed shipments of Hopper chips to China have ended. Gross margin is expected to dip to 71–72% next quarter as the company ramps its newest products.

This was the first earnings report since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on November 12, and the leadership story dominated the call as much as the numbers. Kress, the company’s chief financial officer since 2013 and now interim CEO, told investors “the momentum is real.” Hours earlier, new board chairman Harvey Jones said the board unanimously accepted Huang’s resignation and does not intend to reinstate him, after Huang said he would be honored to return. The board also created a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee, its first major governance move since the resignation. The Wall Street Journal reported the Securities and Exchange Commission is examining Nvidia’s disclosures, and Huang has agreed to testify before three House committees on December 1.

2 Why it matters

First, Nvidia is the bellwether for the entire AI trade, and this report says demand is still outrunning supply. A miss would have rattled every AI stock and every data center builder. Instead, revenue doubled year over year for a company already doing more than $100 billion a quarter, and the $130 billion forecast says the next quarter will be bigger still. The AI buildout, for now, is not slowing down.

Second, the leadership question is now the story, not the numbers. Kress passed her first test with investors, but she is interim, and the board has hired a search firm for a permanent replacement. The board’s public refusal to bring Huang back closes the comeback campaign that had gathered support from employees, investors, and even the president — and it puts the company’s governance under a microscope, with an SEC examination, a congressional appearance in December, and a new export-control committee all landing in the same week.

Revenue doubled in a quarter. The question now is not whether the chips sell — it is who runs the company that sells them.

Third, China is now a permanent zero in Nvidia’s model, not a temporary dip. Licensed Hopper shipments have stopped, the forecast assumes no data center revenue from China, and Washington’s new rule closing the “cloud loophole” tightens the screws further. Nvidia is growing at a triple-digit rate without its second-biggest market — which says a lot about demand elsewhere, and a lot about how the US-China tech split is now priced into the world’s most valuable chip company.

3 What to watch next

  • The market reaction Watch how Nvidia shares trade when the market opens Wednesday, and whether the strength spreads to the rest of the AI trade — or stays a one-company story after Tuesday’s broad market selloff.
  • The CEO search Watch who the board picks as permanent chief executive. The search firm is hired, the board has ruled out Huang, and the choice will shape Nvidia’s strategy for years.
  • The SEC probe Watch what the SEC examination turns up, and what Huang says under oath on December 1 about Nvidia’s disclosures and its chip exports to China.
  • Vera Rubin ramp Watch whether the newest systems ship on schedule and whether gross margin stabilizes after the guided dip to 71–72% next quarter.

Java4Shell: an AI-found flaw in Java’s core lets attackers run code on servers with no login — A flaw in OpenJDK’s optimized Base64 decoder lets a malformed input as simple as 64 letter As followed by an equals sign corrupt memory and open a path to remote code execution. Calif says it found the bug using OpenAI’s GPT-6 Cyber model, Oracle patched it the same day, and the US cybersecurity agency is urging organizations to patch immediately. — The Register

A faulty internal update knocks out iCloud, the App Store, and Apple Pay for hours — Apple services went down on Tuesday after an internal update went wrong, locking users out of iCloud and disrupting the App Store, Apple Pay, and Apple Music. Outage tracker Downdetector logged about 2,300 Apple Pay complaints at the peak. Apple said the issue was not a cyberattack, and most services were back by the afternoon. — USA Today

Microsoft Ignite: Copilot becomes the new OS for work, with an autopilot agent and seven new AI models — Satya Nadella called Copilot the new operating system for work, opening with an apology for last week’s Microsoft 365 outage. The rebuilt Copilot adds Workspaces, a long-running Autopilot agent, and the Microsoft IQ context layer, plus seven new MAI models and GitHub Copilot Fusion routing coding tasks to local models on developers’ machines. — Windows Experience Blog

OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, its cheapest frontier models yet, with a critical cyber risk rating — Astra is OpenAI’s most capable model ever for cyber work, jumping 22 points over its predecessor on the company’s internal ExploitBench test, and its price fell 20% to $8 per million input tokens. Both models are rated at the Critical level for cybersecurity risk under OpenAI’s own framework, launching with tighter safeguards. — ToTechie

Alibaba launches Qwen4-Max, a 2.4 trillion parameter model that costs a third as much per task — The mixture-of-experts model activates only 71 billion parameters per token, with native vision and a one-million-token context window. Alibaba says it cuts the cost per task by about 60% against its predecessor, and independent tracker Artificial Analysis scores it second among Chinese models. — Alibaba Cloud

Amazon sells $5 billion of data centers to Blackstone and leases them back to fund the AI buildout — Blackstone will buy a 75% stake in two joint ventures owning 13 newly built data center buildings with 647 megawatts of capacity, while Amazon keeps 25% and leases everything back for up to 32 years. The sale frees up cash as Amazon spends about $220 billion on capital projects this year. — Blackstone
