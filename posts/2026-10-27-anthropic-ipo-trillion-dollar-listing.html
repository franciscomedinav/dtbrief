Anthropic Is Filing to Go Public at Up to $2 Trillion. Here’s What That Actually Means.

The maker of Claude filed its IPO paperwork on Monday, aiming to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT as soon as the week before Thanksgiving — with a valuation that would make it one of the biggest listings in history.

1 What happened

Anthropic unveiled its IPO paperwork on Monday, filing a 400-page prospectus with the Securities and Exchange Commission that puts it on track to go public as soon as the week before Thanksgiving. The company plans to trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, with a valuation expected to land between $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion.

In plain English: the maker of Claude is about to sell shares to the public for the first time. An S-1 is the document a company files when it wants to go public — hundreds of pages laying out how it makes money, what it spends, and what could go wrong. The “valuation” is the price tag the market puts on the whole company: at $2 trillion, Anthropic would be worth roughly as much as the biggest public companies on Earth, despite being a private startup most people know simply as “the Claude company.”

The numbers inside are eye-popping in both directions. The filing shows explosive revenue growth — one analysis found revenue grew eleven-fold — but also a first-half net loss of $80.7 billion, largely driven by a non-operating convertible-bond item, while the operating loss narrowed to $693 million. Anthropic also warned, in unusually blunt language, that AI could lead to new bioweapons, destabilize the geopolitical order, and evolve beyond human understanding and control.

2 Why it matters

First, this is the AI boom’s biggest test in public markets. Until now, the AI race has been funded by private investors and tech giants writing enormous checks behind closed doors. An IPO forces Anthropic to open its books to everyone — and forces the public market to decide what a frontier AI company is actually worth. The answer will echo across the whole sector.

The filing warns AI could evolve beyond human understanding and control — and asks the public to buy in anyway.

Second, the outcome sets the pace for everyone else. Analysts say a strong debut could trigger a wave of AI listings, with OpenAI’s own IPO next in line, while a stumble could delay those plans and force investors to rethink AI stock valuations across the board. Meta’s $95 billion stock offering the same day shows the whole industry is reaching for public capital at once.

Third, the losses show what the AI buildout actually costs. Even the most admired AI company in the world is burning money at a scale that would sink almost any other business — mostly on computing power. The bet investors are making is that the revenue growth eventually outruns the compute bill. This IPO is the first time that bet gets priced in public.

3 What to watch next

  • The pricing The filing doesn’t yet say how many shares will be sold or at what price. Watch for those details — and whether the final valuation lands near $1.5 trillion or $2 trillion.
  • The debut If the listing happens the week before Thanksgiving as expected, the first days of trading will be the market’s verdict on the whole AI trade. A pop would green-light a wave of listings; a flop would chill them.
  • OpenAI’s turn Analysts say a poor Anthropic debut could delay OpenAI’s listing. Watch whether OpenAI moves up or pushes back its own plans after Anthropic prices.
  • The warnings The prospectus’s blunt warnings about bioweapons and loss of control are unusually stark for an IPO document. Watch how regulators and investors react to a company disclosing those risks while asking the public to buy in.
