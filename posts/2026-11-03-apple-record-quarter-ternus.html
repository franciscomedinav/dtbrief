Apple Posted a Record $114.5 Billion Quarter. The New Boss Passed His First Test — Mostly.

In John Ternus's first earnings report as CEO, Apple beat expectations on revenue and profit — but China revenue and iPad sales missed, and the company cannot make enough of its new foldable iPhone Duo.

1 What happened

Apple reported record September-quarter revenue of $114.5 billion, up twelve percent from a year earlier, in chief executive John Ternus's first earnings report since he took over from Tim Cook in September. Earnings per share came in at $2.10, beating expectations, and net income reached $30.71 billion. For the full fiscal year, Apple posted record revenue of $479 billion.

The beat was driven by the iPhone and the Mac. iPhone revenue hit a September-quarter record of $57.58 billion, up seventeen percent, while Mac sales jumped twenty-four percent to $10.79 billion, helped by Apple's own chips. Services, the company's high-margin engine, delivered another record at $31.57 billion. But not everything cleared the bar: China revenue of $17.54 billion missed forecasts, iPad sales of $6.23 billion came in light, and Ternus confirmed the new iPhone Duo is supply constrained, saying Apple cannot yet say when supply and demand will balance. Shares fell nearly three percent in after-hours trading as investors digested the mixed outlook.

2 Why it matters

First, this was Ternus's debut, and the market was watching for more than numbers. The longtime hardware chief has already overseen Apple's first foldable phone, its first smart home hub, and its first touchscreen laptops, all of which began shipping in the current quarter. A clean beat on revenue and profit gives him credibility — but the after-hours slide shows investors are grading the outlook, not just the quarter.

A record quarter, a three-percent selloff: the market is pricing the future, not the past.

Second, memory costs are the story to watch. Apple warned of slower growth and lower margins in the December quarter as surging memory chip prices bite — the same cost pressure squeezing the whole industry. The company expects record holiday-quarter revenue, but the margin squeeze is why a record quarter still sent the stock down.

Third, for shoppers, the Duo shortage is the practical takeaway. Apple's most talked-about new product is the hardest to buy, and the company will not promise when that changes. If you want one for the holidays, expect waits — and if you are weighing an upgrade, the iPhone 18 Pro, not the Duo, is the model Apple can actually ship at scale right now.

3 What to watch next

  • The Duo wait Watch whether Apple can ramp foldable production before the holidays — and whether it gives a date for supply catching up with demand.
  • Memory margins The December quarter will show how much of the memory price surge Apple absorbs versus passes on. Watch gross margin, not just revenue.
  • China recovery China revenue missed forecasts even as Apple Intelligence arrives there on Alibaba's Qwen model. Watch whether that launch moves the needle next quarter.
  • AI monetization On the call, Apple hinted at revenue opportunities from third-party AI apps and partnerships. Watch for specifics — that could be the next growth story for services.
