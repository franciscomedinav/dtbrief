Jensen Huang Is Out.

Nvidia’s co-founder resigned Thursday after admitting to affairs, one with a suspected Chinese spy. Now Congress wants him to testify, the stock has slid below four trillion dollars in value, and the AI boom’s central company has no permanent leader.

1 What happened

Nvidia announced on Thursday that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, chief executive, and director, effective immediately. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim chief executive, and Harvey C. Jones is the board’s first-ever chair. The resignation came four days after the board opened an investigation into Huang’s conduct, and hours after Reuters reported a third, previously undisclosed relationship with a Nvidia employee. Shares fell 7.8 percent in premarket trading and closed the day down 4.3 percent.

The scandal built fast. On Sunday, Huang told staff in a letter that he had been unfaithful to his wife, apologizing for violating his family’s and his employees’ trust. The woman at the center of the espionage allegations is Alice Huang, a top executive at Singapore-based data-center company Megaspeed International, which the United States and Singapore are scrutinizing over allegations it moved billions of dollars in export-restricted Nvidia chips to China. The Justice Department is now leading multiple investigations involving her, and China has denied the spy allegations. On Wednesday, the New York Times reported a second affair, with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s vice president of health care, who reports directly to him. Nvidia’s code of conduct warns against personal relationships between a manager and a subordinate.

Washington moved in on both sides. Democrats in Congress launched an investigation into Nvidia, writing a scathing letter about Huang’s reported scheme with President Trump. Three Republican House committee chairmen had already asked Huang to brief them on November 18 about Alice Huang and Megaspeed, and after the resignation Huang agreed to testify before Congress by Thanksgiving, behind closed doors, after senior lawmakers threatened to subpoena him. President Trump floated Apple chairman Tim Cook as a possible successor to Huang.

The resignation also puts Nvidia’s open-source commitments in question. Huang had championed open-weight models and the company’s Hugging Face acquisition, and the company’s support for open source AI is now uncertain under interim leadership. Hugging Face’s chief executive vowed to keep fighting for open source AI after Huang’s exit. Nvidia reports third-quarter results on Tuesday, November 17 — Kress’s first earnings call in the top job, with the leadership question hanging over every number.

2 Why it matters

First, this is a leadership crisis at the company the entire AI boom runs on. Nvidia dominates AI processors, and Huang has been its face and strategist for more than three decades. The board’s own proxy statement warns the company is highly dependent on its longstanding executive team, and Huang has said he does not believe in succession planning. Huang kept about sixty direct reports, and there is no obvious successor. The market is pricing that in: Nvidia has lost about $300 billion in market value since the board announced its investigation on Sunday.

Second, the scandal is two stories at once: governance and national security. The Powell affair is a governance problem — a chief executive involved with a direct report, against the company’s own policy. The Alice Huang affair is a national-security problem: a CEO who lobbied the president to let Nvidia ship chips to China while involved with a business partner US officials believe is a Chinese intelligence asset. The investigation asks whether secrets were compromised; the resignation answers the governance question but leaves the security one open.

One affair is a governance problem. The other is a national-security problem. Now both are a leadership problem.

Third, the fallout reaches beyond Nvidia. The AI trade has been wobbling for weeks: a Federal Reserve governor warned this week that disappointing AI returns could trigger a large correction, OpenAI just closed a funding round at a lower valuation than it first sought, and Anthropic prices its giant IPO next week. The boom’s central chipmaker losing its founder-CEO in a scandal is exactly the kind of shock that tests whether the market still believes.

3 What to watch next

  • Nov 17 — earnings Nvidia reports third-quarter results on Tuesday. Watch the numbers — and who speaks for the company, and whether Kress addresses the leadership question at all.
  • By Thanksgiving — testimony Huang agreed to testify before Congress by Thanksgiving, behind closed doors, with the session expected to be recorded and transcribed. Watch what he says about Alice Huang and Megaspeed, and whether Democrats’ investigation turns formal.
  • The permanent search Watch who the board picks — an insider like Kress, an outsider, or a name like Tim Cook — and how long the search takes. Nvidia’s bylaws let a majority of its 11 directors remove a CEO with or without cause; picking the next one is the harder job.
  • Nov 18–19 — Anthropic IPO Anthropic prices November 18 and starts trading November 19 at up to a $1.4 trillion valuation. Watch whether investors still pay up for AI after a week like this — it is the first real test of the AI trade since Huang resigned.

The rest of today’s briefing

Nonprofits sue OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta, and SpaceX to halt frontier AI development — A group of nonprofits sued the five companies on Thursday, asking a San Francisco court to block further development of the most advanced AI models. The suit cites the companies’ own leaders’ warnings about AI’s potential to cause the destruction of civilization, and accuses them of business practices that create a public nuisance. Meta declined to comment, and the Trump administration has so far declined to pursue large-scale AI regulation. — Reuters

A Microsoft 365 outage knocks out Outlook for hours and is still not fully fixed — A widespread Microsoft outage disrupted Outlook, Microsoft 365, Copilot, and other services for about ten hours on Thursday, with Downdetector recording a peak of about 129,000 Outlook problem reports. Microsoft traced the problem to Exchange Online, and as of Friday morning its status page still showed service degradation, with recovery partial and not sustained. Microsoft has not published a root cause and says it is not aware of any malicious activity. — USA Today

Anthropic sets its IPO price range at $70 to $78 a share, valuing the company at up to $1.4 trillion — Anthropic set its IPO price range at $70 to $78 a share for 825 million Class A shares, which would raise as much as $64.35 billion and value the Claude maker at up to $1.4 trillion. The shares are expected to price on November 18 and begin trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on November 19, with seven suppliers — AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, and TSMC — agreeing to a $26 billion private placement. The top of the range is about 30 percent below the $2 trillion valuation investors discussed in August. — Decrypt

Meta’s Muse can now place calls to US businesses, but only if you pay — Meta’s AI agent Muse can now place calls to US businesses on behalf of its users, after testing the feature since September, but only paid subscribers have access. Plans start at $20 a month, and Meta says Muse now has more than 10 million users. Reuters reported that Meta tested using human contractors to place some Muse calls when the AI kept failing at them. — TechCrunch

Google rolls out Gemini 4 Hydrogen in Search, its biggest model upgrade to Search yet — Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Thursday and is rolling it out globally across Google Search, calling it the biggest model upgrade to Search so far. Hydrogen is now the default model in AI Mode and is being used for the hardest questions in AI Overviews, handling multi-part questions, typos, and specific requests. Google says it completes tasks two and a half times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, though those are the company’s own claims. — Search Engine Journal

A cyberattack on Fireworks AI exposed Notion customers’ chat transcripts — A cyberattack at Fireworks AI exposed some customer data after an attacker breached its cloud provider to reach a development system, taking billing information, datasets, and, for some accounts, secrets and incomplete model weights. Notion said the breach affected around 1 percent of its customers, including chat transcripts of Notion AI users of its Insights feature. Notion disconnected from Fireworks on November 3 and told affected customers to rotate any passwords or API keys they pasted into Notion AI. — TechCrunch

HubSpot co-founder Brian Halligan returns as chief executive — HubSpot said on Thursday that co-founder Brian Halligan would return as chief executive, replacing Yamini Rangan, who steps down effective November 12 but stays through December 1. Halligan co-founded HubSpot in 2005 and served as CEO for 16 years, leading it through its IPO. Under Rangan, customers grew from 135,000 to more than 311,000, and revenue from $1.3 billion in 2021 to an expected $3.7 billion in 2026. — Reuters

Nebius lays out $40 billion in planned spending for 2027, and its shares sink — Neocloud company Nebius laid out $40 billion in planned spending for 2027 on its earnings call Thursday, far more than Wall Street expected, and its shares fell 17 percent. It plans to fund the buildout with customer prepayments and loans backed by customer contracts, but its biggest customer, Microsoft, is paying for capacity at a New Jersey data center now months behind schedule under a $19.4 billion contract. Third-quarter revenue was $981 million, up 571 percent from a year earlier. — TechCrunch

SpaceX and Tesla merger talks are advancing, with a deal possible as soon as December — SpaceX and Tesla are advancing merger talks, and a deal could be announced as early as December, Bloomberg reported on Thursday. Combined, the two companies are worth roughly $3.5 trillion, which would make it the largest corporate deal ever, though the announcement could slip into early 2027. Bank of America expects SpaceX to buy Tesla with stock, with Elon Musk holding about 56 percent of the combined voting power. — CoinDesk
